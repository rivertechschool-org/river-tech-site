Welcome to our sixth year

Glad your family is part of it.

Dear parents,

Our doors are about to open on our sixth year, and we couldn’t be more glad your family is part of it. Here at River Tech, we’re building something unique—a learning environment that nurtures community leaders who are tech-savvy and artistic, all while being grounded in Christian faith and Idaho values.

We’re growing

This fall we’re bigger than we’ve ever been—nearly double last year’s size, with about 130 students. We didn’t chase that; families came, and told other families. It’s humbling, and we’re grateful.

We’ll keep you posted all year long—what’s coming, what’s changed, and what your kids have been up to. This page is where it all gathers.

With gratitude,
Dan & Mary Hegelund
Founders & Principal, River Tech School

First day & logistics

First Day of School: September 1st.

When your child starts

Your first day depends on your program:

  • Full-time: classes begin Tuesday, September 1.
  • À la carte homeschool: you start on the first day you signed up for that week—if Friday is your day, then Friday, September 4 is your first day.
  • Auditioning for Aladdin? Auditions are Monday, August 31—and for anyone trying out, that Monday is their first day. Come ready.

Where & when

  • Where: The Heart, 927 E Polston Ave, Post Falls.
  • Arrival: full-time students arrive by 8:30 (we open with assembly); à la carte homeschoolers by 10:25 on your days.
  • Dismissal: 2:35 for most students; 2:30 for high schoolers and homeschoolers.

Parent meetings on the first day

On Tuesday, September 1, we’ll hold a parent meeting from 8:30 to 9:30 a.m. That afternoon at 2:45, we’ll hold a second one just for high school families. Both are optional—anything we cover, we’ll also put in an email and here on the website, so you won’t miss it if you can’t be there.

Tuition & billing

Quarterly payments are now the new default.

Your first invoice for the year arrives through FreshBooks, from Faithful Five, our nonprofit. (If you see “Faithful Five” on a bank statement or receipt, that’s us—nothing to worry about.)

We’ve moved to quarterly billing as our standard—it’s the simplest for everyone and carries our best rate. When your invoice comes, you can set it to pay automatically, and the rest of the year takes care of itself.

Idaho tax credit or a different arrangement

If you’re using the Idaho Parental Choice Tax Credit or need a different arrangement, just reply to us and we’ll sort it out together.

Paying by check

We encourage families to pay by invoice—card or bank transfer is the simplest and fastest, and it’s how most of our families pay. Checks are still welcome, with one small change: we can now accept them on the quarterly and annual plans only, not monthly. If you pay monthly by check today, you have two easy options—move to a quarterly or annual plan to keep paying by check, or switch to invoice on the monthly plan. Any questions, just reply and we’ll help.

Dress code

Modest, professional, distraction-free.

River Tech’s daily-wear standard supports learning, safety, focus, modesty, and professionalism while promoting school identity and parity among students. Our emphasis is on character over appearance (1 Peter 3:3–4). Daily wear is flexible within these standards.

Helpful test: would you feel confident sending your student to a job interview in this outfit?

Detailed guide

Tops. Collared polos, henleys, blouses, button-downs, knit tops, t-shirts, crewneck/V-neck sweaters, and cardigans. Sleeveless is OK with straps at least 2.5 inches wide and fitted arm openings. Opaque fabrics; necklines stay professional with cleavage covered.

Bottoms. Chinos/khakis, trousers, joggers (P.E. only), and neat denim. Shorts, skirts, skorts, and dresses at fingertip length or longer. Distressing kept refined: any hole no larger than a half-dollar, and never exposing skin above fingertip length.

Coverage & fit. Midriff and seat stay covered during normal activity (walking, sitting, standing, bending, stretching). Garments fit securely at the waist—loose is fine, overly baggy is not. Undergarments stay fully covered.

Footwear. Clean, secure shoes suited to indoor and outdoor movement. Closed-toe shoes are required for labs, shop, and certain field trips. For P.E.: running shoes and athletic socks.

Layers & outerwear. In class: sweaters, cardigans, light jackets, vests, and school-spirit outerwear. Sunglasses, bandanas, scarves, and heavy coats are for arrival, dismissal, and outdoors—store them during class unless needed for health.

Accessories & jewelry. Subtle, safe, and distraction-free. Piercing jewelry in ears only (modest studs or hoops). No spikes, sharp hardware, or unsafe accessories. Belts, watches, and simple bracelets/necklaces are welcome when subtle and safe.

Hair & grooming. Clean, neat, and professional. No carved names, symbols, or numbers. One natural hair color; natural-colored highlights and lowlights are allowed. Facial hair neatly trimmed.

Makeup & nails. Moderate and well-kept, suited to a professional and educational setting.

Graphics, messages & symbols. Allowed when school-appropriate and aligned with River Tech values. Not allowed: violence, drugs/alcohol/tobacco, profanity, sexual content, or demeaning themes.

P.E. & active wear. Required: running shoes; athletic shorts, sweats, or joggers; an athletic top. Length and coverage standards still apply. Tie back long hair for safety.

Enforcement

An isolated dress correction is a minor violation—handled quietly by the teacher with an on-the-spot fix where possible. Repeated disregard becomes a pattern concern and moves to an administrative response.

Student devices

Minimum guidelines so you can plan a purchase.

As a tech school, a powerful-enough device should be a priority. Simple coding and digital arts work on a tablet; more advanced coding and 3D modeling need a laptop, so the right choice depends on your child’s grade.

  • Elementary: a tablet is all you need.
  • 5th–6th grade: a laptop is recommended, but a tablet is still fine.
  • 7th grade and up: a laptop is required—students use programs like Blender and Unity that a tablet can’t run.

Full-time students and Friday students bring their device daily, charged each evening. Homeschoolers who attend only Monday, Tuesday, and/or Thursday don’t need a device. (If your child misbehaves, please choose a consequence other than withholding the school device—it’s a vital learning tool.)

If you’re buying a tablet

An iPad from 2022 or newer is recommended; a current Android tablet like the Samsung Galaxy Tab A9+ also works.

  • At least 64GB of storage (128GB preferred)
  • A current iPadOS or Android version
  • We don’t recommend Amazon Fire tablets—they don’t reliably run the apps we use

If you’re buying a laptop — Mac

  • Apple-silicon MacBook, M1 or newer
  • At least 16GB unified memory
  • At least 256GB storage (512GB preferred)

If you’re buying a laptop — Windows

  • A recent Windows 11 laptop
  • At least 16GB of RAM
  • Dedicated NVIDIA RTX 2050/3050-class graphics or better
  • At least 256GB SSD (512GB preferred)

What to avoid

8GB RAM configurations, Chromebooks, Intel-based MacBooks, and Windows laptops with only basic integrated graphics.

A refurbished laptop can be a great value from a reputable seller with a clear return policy and warranty. If you’d like, send us a listing before you buy and we’ll confirm the configuration is a good fit. Unsure whether a device is powerful enough? Email Luke at email@lukehegelund.com for advice.

Handbook & expectations

One standard, for every family.

Who our guidelines apply to

Everything in the Handbook—our culture, dress code, policies, and daily schedule—applies equally to all of our students, whether full-time or part-time / homeschool. We hold one standard for everyone who takes part in school activities.

Please read the Handbook. Parents should read it in full. Students in 7th grade and up are encouraged to read it too; younger children don’t need to, though the expectations still apply to them.

If, after reading through it all, you find these guidelines aren’t the right fit for your family, we understand—and we’d gently ask that you let us know as soon as possible rather than waiting, so we can offer the spot to a family on our wait pool. We say this warmly: we’d love to have you, and we want every family who stays to be all-in.

Idaho roots

River Tech reflects the character of our region. Idaho runs on faith, family, hard work, and time spent outdoors, and so do we. Kids grow best when school and home share the same direction, and we want parents to know ours before they enroll.

A Christian school

Our staff are Christians, and the shape of the school day reflects that. We open each morning with worship, prayer, and a short devotional. Faith runs through the day rather than being set aside during classes.

We welcome families from many different churches, and we don’t promote one denomination over another. C.S. Lewis had a phrase for this—“mere Christianity,” a shared foundation that honors the core of the faith while leaving the details to churches and parents.

A small school, on purpose

Our classes are small, about fifteen students, because that’s the size where a school can actually know a child. Every teacher knows every student by name, knows what they’re working on, and notices when something is off. We lean on parents, too—for volunteer days, guest teaching, field-trip help, and honest feedback about what’s working and what isn’t. River Tech is a community, and your family is part of it.

What we stand for, and what we don’t

We stand for God, family, church, community, personal responsibility, patriotism, merit, grit, and entrepreneurship.

We don’t teach gender ideology, critical race theory, victim mentality, or cancel culture. We are committed instead to a stable, time-tested framework that fosters character, strength, clarity, and wisdom. Students are addressed as boys and girls. Our teachers prepare students for adult life, including courtship, marriage, and parenting—but our students do not engage in idle talk about crushes, romantic relationships, or LGBTQ.

Grit

Kids need challenge to become capable adults. A child should never experience abuse, harm, or bullying, but they should absolutely experience discomfort, frustration, and the feeling of something genuinely hard, in order to become resilient under pressure.

We spend about twenty minutes each day on drill and calisthenics. Drill teaches focus, posture, and the discipline of moving as a group; calisthenics builds real physical readiness. Once a week we head to the park, and every three weeks or so we take a field trip—often something physical like wall climbing, trampoline jumping, or roller skating. We celebrate the wins, too, from birthdays and finished projects to end-of-term parties.

We’re a technology school, but we believe real-life experiences are foundational for healthy growth. That’s why we balance tech classes with performing arts, liberal arts, and life skills—and why we protect the things technology can’t replace: family time, real friendships, time in nature, creative work, and a growing faith.

How we teach hard things

Our classrooms take on the great stories, histories, and ideas of the world, including the difficult ones. War, mythology, and competing worldviews are central to understanding the human story, and we don’t flinch from them. We teach them with Christian commentary and a conservative frame, so that students come away with moral clarity rather than moral confusion.

Nondiscrimination

As a Christian and conservative school, River Tech favors Christianity and conservatism in our education—for example, in hiring we are more likely to hire a Christian teacher who shares the school’s values. That said, River Tech is committed to nondiscrimination in relation to biological race, sex, age, handicap, and other human differences. We foster an atmosphere of respect for all people, regardless of worldview, in harmony with Jesus’ instruction, “love your neighbor as yourself” (Mark 12:31).

Attendance

Each morning, a guardian must check in their child with a teacher in the foyer. Each teacher keeps a list of who is allowed to pick up a child; during the first week, email learn@rivertech.me or tell a teacher to add names and relationships to the list.

By Idaho, every River Tech student is considered a homeschool student, so parents are responsible for their child’s attendance. Regular attendance is essential for achievement. For high schoolers, attendance is part of the grade—one-eighth of the total grade in each class—and they are expected to attend as often as they are able.

Absences

If a student will be absent, please email learn@rivertech.me on or before the day of the absence, before 8:30 a.m. If there has been no contact and a child isn’t present when expected, a teacher will try to reach the parents. Please schedule doctor and dentist appointments before or after school hours where possible.

If a child is sick (fever, cough, ache, or fatigue), notify us and keep them home for at least 24 hours after the fever is gone without fever-reducing medicine. A fever is 100°F or higher. We will send sick children home, and you’ll need to come pick them up.

Assembly

Each morning, full-time students gather for assembly—a mix of worship, a devotional, prayer, and the Pledge of Allegiance or National Anthem.

Closed campus

River Tech is a closed campus. Doors are kept locked except for morning check-in and afternoon pickup. Drop-off is 8:10–8:30; if you arrive late, the doors will be locked. If you need access at another time, arrange it in advance and call the school when you arrive—someone will open the door at 9:35, 10:25, 11:05, or during lunch (11:45–1:00). High schoolers may leave during lunch with parent permission; it’s a privilege that can be revoked.

Dismissal times

  • High School (9–12): 2:30 p.m.
  • Elementary and Middle (through 8): 2:35 p.m.
  • The front door is the only exit for all students.

General rules (all grades)

  • Authorized pickup only: students are released to parents/guardians or adults on the Approved Pickup List.
  • Families may designate regular pick-upers.
  • Non-regular pick-upers walk to the front door to state their identity and the student; teachers verify and may request photo ID.
  • Same-day changes: notify the school in writing (email/text) as early as possible that day.
  • Photo ID may be requested; custody orders on file are followed.

By grade

Elementary (1–4): a parent or authorized adult collects the child at the front entrance; teachers verify and record the handoff.

Middle school (5–8): front-door release with identification. If a teacher clearly recognizes a parent or regular pick-uper, the student may walk to the vehicle; if not, the adult parks and walks to the front door to verify.

High school (9–12): independent release at 2:30 via the front door—students may leave on their own. Families wanting parent-only pickup should email learn@rivertech.me.

Late pickup & early pull-outs

There’s a 15-minute grace period after the last exit (ending 2:50). After that, students wait with Dan or Mary in the staff offices; please notify the school if you’ll be late. Habitual lateness may prompt a courtesy conference. Early pull-outs are allowed with clear written notice on the same day, at the latest.

Homework

Each day a student is expected to complete a set amount of schoolwork; whatever isn’t finished in class is finished that night at home. Elementary and middle schoolers have a weekly vocabulary test and write a monthly essay. High schoolers receive additional homework. Repeated failure to complete assigned work is a responsibility concern under the Code of Conduct.

Make-up work & assessments

If a child is absent, they catch up at home; teachers may schedule a meeting if a child falls too far behind. At the end of the 1st and 3rd quarters, parents of students 12 and older are invited to a parent-teacher meeting; parents of students 11 and younger are invited at the end of the 2nd quarter.

Class changes

Students or parents may request to move into a higher or lower level for a current class. The request is discussed with the teacher and the parent.

Four-day students

Four-day students attend 80% of the time, so some of the full curriculum is missed. We place the most essential subjects on Tuesday–Thursday where practical, but it remains the parent’s responsibility to teach the remaining part. Parents are encouraged to bring work home and continue their studies; teachers are always happy to advise what to cover.

Homeschoolers

River Tech offers flexible enrichment for homeschool families—choose 1 to 4 days per week, with tuition scaling accordingly. Homeschool programs generally serve students ages 9–16 unless otherwise specified. All classes are taught with Christian commentary and a conservative moral framework, by a team with college degrees in their subject areas.

Cubbies & desks

Each full-time student is assigned a cubby or locker for class supplies, since desks are shared. Cubbies and lockers are school property and may be inspected. Don’t keep valuables in them—the school isn’t responsible for lost or stolen items. Students are responsible for the cleanliness and condition of their cubby or locker, including removing any writing, graffiti, or stickers.

Food & commons

Parents provide lunches—ideally a balanced, mostly cold meal with vegetables, fruit, protein, and whole grains. Microwaves are available, but we can’t refrigerate, so pack an ice pack. Store food in backpacks, not cubbies. Please skip desserts. Drinks are limited to milk, water, and 100% juice, and every student brings a full (preferably reusable) water bottle daily. Once a week each age group eats lunch at the park and needs a cold lunch. Please tell staff about any dietary restrictions or health conditions.

Lost & found

The lost & found box is in the hallway at the top of the stairs. Please check it weekly, and label valuables with your child’s name.

Phones & devices

Students are discouraged from bringing a cell phone; if they do, it stays in the backpack all day. A phone used during the day is confiscated until dismissal. Students are, however, required to bring their own tablet or laptop for their grade level. Parental controls can be added but aren’t required, since the school runs its own safety network. Devices are for school use and shouldn’t carry non-educational apps that aren’t school-approved. (Full device specs are on the Student Devices tab.)

School currency (Gold)

For full-time students, the school uses a currency called Gold (RTC) to recognize effort and good contribution. Gold is a recognition system, not a discipline system—it’s earned for positive contribution and faithful stewardship, and it is never deducted as a punishment. Eligibility for certain privileges may depend on responsibility and a positive behavioral trajectory.

“For whom the Lord loveth he correcteth.” — Proverbs 3:12

River Tech exists to cultivate bright minds and disciplined character. Discipline is not primarily about punishment—it is about formation. Correction is an act of care; standards are an act of love. When standards are violated, behavior is corrected calmly and consistently.

The RIVER values

  • Responsibility — we do what is expected promptly, carefully, and without being chased.
  • Integrity — we tell the truth, own our actions, and do what is right even when no one is watching.
  • Voice — we use words, tone, and timing that are respectful, clean, and fitting for the moment.
  • Excellence — we complete our work and carry ourselves with professionalism, care, and purpose.
  • Respect — we honor people, property, time, and authority through our conduct and attitude.

The student pledge

Students pledge to: submit to civil authority and its laws (which rules out tobacco, alcohol, marijuana, vaping, illegal drugs, gambling, and shoplifting); submit to those in authority over them; respect and honor others; respect the property of others (and report accidental damage immediately); conduct themselves with honor and integrity in word; use wholesome speech that respects God, Christianity, and His Church; be modest in dress and appearance; use technology appropriately (only school-approved apps during school hours); and inform a teacher or administrator if they learn of anyone threatening harm to themselves or others.

Standards of conduct

Students are expected to show responsibility (prompt obedience, composure in transitions, care for shared spaces, accountability), integrity (truthfulness, ownership, honest work), voice (no profanity or mocking; respectful tone), excellence (careful work, neat appearance, proper device use), and respect (dignity toward others, response to authority, hands to themselves, no bullying).

Discipline policy

River Tech uses a clear two-tier model; correction is calm, brief, and consistent.

Minor violations (teacher-managed) are isolated departures—running indoors, talking during instruction, minor dress corrections, first-time device misuse, mild disrespect, careless messes, incomplete work. Teachers respond with immediate correction, a reset where applicable, brief restorative action, and documentation if it repeats.

Major violations (administrative) include blatant defiance, physical aggression, harassment or bullying, directed profanity, threats, serious device misuse, academic dishonesty, theft or intentional damage, leaving class or campus without permission, serious disrespect, or an established pattern of minor violations. Responses may include parent contact, detention, restitution, restorative service, loss of privileges, a behavior contract, suspension, or—when necessary—expulsion.

A single mistake does not define a student. Repeated minor violations indicate a trajectory concern, and when a pattern develops, administrative intervention may occur. Restoration is the goal—students may be asked to redo the behavior properly, apologize, repair damage, make restitution, or take part in restorative service.

Progressive intervention & enrollment

River Tech is committed to restoration before removal. Phase 1 corrects minor violations and documents patterns, with informal parent notice. Phase 2 begins when a pattern persists: parents are formally notified, a meeting is scheduled, a written behavior contract may be issued, and a review window (typically 30–45 days) is set. Phase 3 is an enrollment determination if the pattern continues and growth isn’t demonstrated—which may mean a withdrawal recommendation, non-renewal, or expulsion in severe cases.

Immediate removal may skip these phases for physical violence, threats of harm, serious harassment, severe academic dishonesty, weapons, drugs/tobacco/alcohol, or actions that compromise safety. Removal is based on trajectory, responsiveness to correction, and influence on culture—not merely a count of infractions.

Harassment, bullying & threats

Bullying, threats, and harassment—whether sexual, ethnic, racial, age, religious, or handicap-related—will not be tolerated. Students who are bullied or who observe bullying must report it to a teacher immediately. Any threat of bodily harm, regardless of intent or whether it’s “a joke,” is taken very seriously and may lead to suspension or expulsion. These are major violations and involve administration immediately.

Speech & language

Level 1 — avoidable language (e.g., “dang it,” “that’s dumb”): a minor violation, corrected verbally. Level 2 — targeted language (e.g., “you’re stupid,” “shut up”): a minor violation with a stronger correction and documentation; repetition moves to administration. Level 3 — profanity and vulgarity: a major violation with administrative involvement, parent contact, and restorative action.

Devices, plagiarism, weapons, and substances

Devices are tools for learning; first or isolated misuse is a minor violation (teacher correction, device confiscated until day’s end), while serious misuse—accessing prohibited content, bypassing the safety network, installing unapproved apps, or using a device to harass, record, or deceive—is a major violation from the first instance.

Plagiarism—presenting others’ ideas or writing as your own—is not allowed, and AI tools such as ChatGPT are not permitted on assignments unless a teacher says otherwise. Academic dishonesty is a major violation.

Weapons: bringing knives, guns, or objects made as weapons onto campus or to school activities may result in suspension or expulsion (per Idaho Code). Drugs, tobacco, and alcohol: River Tech maintains a drug-, tobacco-, and alcohol-free environment; violations may result in suspension or expulsion and parent notification.

How we handle concerns

We resolve concerns promptly, fairly, and biblically—going first to the person privately, then including one or two others, then bringing it to leadership if still unresolved (Matthew 18:15–17), restoring gently (Galatians 6:1), and speaking the truth in love (Ephesians 4:15). Start small and close, seek understanding, be timely, and respect privacy.

By situation

Student with another student: student speaks with the student privately and proposes a repair; if unresolved, the student goes in person to the shared teacher for mediation; then a teacher-coordinated parent meeting; then the Principal, whose decision is final.

Parent about a student situation: parent emails the shared teacher to schedule a meeting; if unresolved, parent emails the Principal (cc teacher). The Principal’s decision is final.

Student with a teacher: student shares the concern privately (emailing the teacher to schedule if needed); if unresolved, student emails the Principal (cc teacher).

Parent with a teacher: parent emails the teacher to schedule a meeting; if unresolved, parent emails the Principal (cc teacher).

Standards & protections

Use specific facts, assume good intent, keep details confidential to those involved, and let staff document the concern and next steps. Email is the default for scheduling (teacher first, Principal when escalated). For immediate risk, harassment, discrimination, self-harm, abuse, or other legally reportable concerns, skip directly to the Principal (learn@rivertech.me) and authorities as required. No one is penalized for raising a good-faith concern; the Principal is the final authority.

Emergency preparation

River Tech works with local government, law enforcement, and emergency-management officials to monitor safety and respond quickly. We maintain Safe School Plans, run emergency drills, and require a clean criminal-record and reference check for every teacher. Several staff are first-responder certified (Red Cross Adult and Pediatric First Aid/CPR/AED). Parents are responsible for keeping current primary and secondary contact information on file. If a guardian can’t pick up a child after an emergency, the child remains under staff supervision until an authorized adult arrives.

School closure

If the school must close, parents are notified by email and it’s posted on the website and social media. We generally follow the Post Falls School District’s closure decisions.

Swimming will be limited to the left corner of the designated swimming area, where the water does not get very deep. Students who cannot swim independently must bring a life vest. Without one, they may not go deeper than waist level.

Volunteers needed. Team games take place before noon, followed by swimming and hiking after noon. You may volunteer for the full event or just one portion. We need help with:

  • Leading team games, ball games, and hiking
  • Supervising students in the water and on the playground
  • Food setup and cleanup

To sign up, text Mary at 425-444-2271 with your name, your student’s name and age, your preferred role, and the time you are available.

What to bring

  • A large water bottle filled with fresh water
  • A modest swimsuit and towel—no bikinis; two-piece swimsuits must provide adequate coverage
  • A cold lunch if there is a fire ban—we will confirm this beforehand

Food contributions. The school will provide hot dogs if BBQs are permitted. Families may contribute side dishes, fruit or vegetables, chips or snacks, ice, and drinks or desserts. For drinks and desserts, please avoid artificial colors, high-fructose corn syrup, and similar additives.

September 16 — Hidden Acres Farm & Student Farmers Market

This is one of our favorite River Tech family events! Students will pick fresh produce at Hidden Acres Farm and bring it back to school to sell at our Student Farmers Market. This hands-on experience teaches students about healthy nutrition, local farming, business, finances, leadership, and teamwork. Families then get to take home fresh produce the students picked that very day!

River Tech covers transportation and produce costs for full-time students. Any proceeds from the Farmers Market go toward the River Tech field trip fund.

Participation. Full-time students will travel in a school bus, van, or teacher’s vehicle and will be supervised by River Tech staff. Part-time homeschool students may participate if a parent provides transportation, a parent supervises them throughout the field trip, and the family pays separately for any produce collected.

Activities. Picking fresh produce at the farm, eating lunch at the farm, and participating in the Student Farmers Market at the end of the school day.

Volunteers needed. We need chaperones who can drive to the farm, supervise small groups as they collect produce, help set up the Farmers Market, and assist students as they handle money and sell their produce.

We’ll meet at the farm at 10:45 a.m. and return to school around 1:30 p.m. to set up the market, which stays open until 3:00 p.m. You may volunteer at the farm, the market, or both. To sign up, text Mary at 425-444-2271.

What to bring — students: a water bottle, a cold lunch, clothes and shoes that can get dirty, and cash if desired (the school will purchase the produce). Parents: cash to purchase produce from the students, and plastic bags for your purchases.

If you have any questions, please email maryhegelund@gmail.com. We’re looking forward to these fun days with our students and are grateful for the parents who help make them possible!

What to prepare

You’re welcome to audition for any role that interests you, and directors may also consider you for additional roles based on your audition. Here’s what to get ready:

Dance. Everyone does a brief dance audition. About 30 seconds of choreography is taught right there during auditions—no preparation needed. Just come ready to learn and move.

Vocal. If you’re auditioning for one of these roles, prepare the matching song:

  • Aladdin — “One Jump Ahead”
  • Jasmine — “Speechless”
  • Genie — “Friend Like Me”
  • Babkak, Omar or Kassim — “High Adventure”

Acting. Prepare the scene for the role you want—all the scenes are below. Memorization isn’t required, but it’s highly encouraged: hunting for lines pulls your focus away from delivery and stage presence.

Aladdin Scene

APPLE VENDOR: “You’d better be able to pay for that!” JASMINE: “Pay?” ALADDIN (improvising): “Wait! My sister didn’t mean any harm! She’s... uh... she’s not feeling herself today.” APPLE VENDOR: “She seems perfectly healthy to me.” JASMINE (acting along, points to APPLE VENDOR): “Why is that camel talking to us?” ALADDIN: “What camel?” JASMINE: “The one in the turban.” ALADDIN: “Uh-oh, she’s getting worse. I’d better get her to the doctor.” (ALADDIN grabs JASMINE’s hand and they walk away, right into RAZOUL and GUARDS.) RAZOUL: “Street rat!” APPLE VENDOR: “She stole from me!” JASMINE: “The camel is mad!” ALADDIN: “This way!”

Jasmine & Sultan Scene

JASMINE: “Father?” SULTAN (instantly doting): “Yes, pumpkin?” (Catches himself.) “What did you say to that poor prince?” JASMINE: “I just told him the truth: that if he marries me, he’ll get an equal partner with an equal say. Why do I even have to marry at all? What’s wrong with a woman running the kingdom?” IAGO (aside): “Talk about living in a fairy tale!” SULTAN: “Jasmine, you’re a princess. The law states that you must marry a prince by the next moon. We must uphold the ancient ways, dearest.” JASMINE: “If I have to marry someone I don’t love, then maybe I don’t want to be a princess anymore.” SULTAN: “It’s not just the law. I won’t be around forever, and I want to make sure you’re taken care of.”

Dalia Scene

DALIA: “What did the Sultan say?” JASMINE: “That if I don’t pick a suitor, he will choose a husband for me.” DALIA: “Oh dear, I don’t see that going well.” JASMINE: “All my life he surrounds me with knowledge of the world and then he walls me up inside this palace! It’s like I’m a prisoner.” DALIA: “In a very comfortable prison.” JASMINE: “Having riches doesn’t mean you have happiness. More than anything, I want to see the world... find love... live my own life. But Father cares more about ancient laws than his own daughter.”

Genie Scene

GENIE (to audience): “Hello....everybody! Did ya miss me?!” ALADDIN: “I think I might be hallucinating...” GENIE: “Hallucinating? Impossible! I may be dreamy, but I’m no hallucination.” ALADDIN: “Who are you?” GENIE: “Who am I? Who am I? Isn’t it obvious? The Genie is here!” ALADDIN: “So you’re from inside the lamp?” GENIE: “No, I’m from Cleveland. Of course I’m from inside the lamp! And you are the master of said lamp, so you get three wishes!” ALADDIN: “You’re gonna give me three wishes?” GENIE: “And I thought the echo in the lamp was bad! Try to keep up, kid. I got a production number to get to!” ALADDIN: “You mean... I can wish for anything I want?” GENIE: “That’s right! But ixnay on wishing for more wishes. You got a name, kid?” ALADDIN: “Aladdin.” GENIE: “Mind if I call you Al... Al? Why don’t you just ruminate whilst I illuminate the poss-o-bilities!”

Jafar & Iago Scene

IAGO: “We’ve got problems, sire! Major problems!” JAFAR: “Have you forgotten how to address me, Iago?!” IAGO: “Oh, Wonderful One! Oh, Fantastic and Powerful One!” JAFAR: “Better. Now, what were you squawking about?” IAGO: “Princess Jasmine has been talking with Prince Abdullah for over an hour.” JAFAR: “An hour?? But if she chooses a suitor, he becomes the next sultan... instead of me!” IAGO: “That’s why I said we got problems!!”

Babkak, Omar & Kassim Scene

BABKAK: “Turbans! Get your red-hot turbans here! We hate to sell them, but we’ve fallen on hungry times!” (To KASSIM) “This is so much better than living in the palace!” KASSIM: “I don’t stay where I’m not appreciated.” (OMAR runs on, hyperventilating.) OMAR: “Kassim! Babkak! Help!!!” KASSIM: “What’s wrong?” OMAR: “Aladdin! Trouble! Arrested! Palace!” BABKAK: “We’ve got to do something!” KASSIM: “I told Aladdin this Prince Ali scam would blow up in his face and now it has.” OMAR: “He might be a little mixed up right now, but is that any reason to betray a friend?” KASSIM: “He said he didn’t need us.” BABKAK: “But if something terrible happens, we’ll never forgive ourselves!” KASSIM: “Okay, okay, you’re right.” OMAR: “So what are we gonna do?” KASSIM (light bulb): “Storm the palace! Who’s up for a little high adventure?”

Razoul Scene

RAZOUL: “I see you have a new partner in crime, street rat.” (The GUARDS re-enter and detain JASMINE.) ALADDIN: “Let her go. She didn’t do anything wrong.” RAZOUL: “Silence! Or I’ll finish you both right here in the street.” JASMINE: “Wait! I command you to stop!” (JASMINE lowers her hood to reveal her tiara.) RAZOUL: “Princess Jasmine!?” (RAZOUL and GUARDS bow.)

Prince Abdullah Scene

SULTAN: “Prince Abdullah!” PRINCE ABDULLAH: “I’ve never encountered such impertinence in all my life!” (JAFAR and IAGO are relieved.) SULTAN: “What did she do?” PRINCE ABDULLAH: “The rumors about your daughter are completely unfounded. I was told she is very amiable!” SULTAN: “She is very amiable.” PRINCE ABDULLAH: “I was told she is very charming.” SULTAN: “She is very charming.” PRINCE ABDULLAH: “I was told she is very obedient.” SULTAN: “She is very charming.” PRINCE ABDULLAH: “I’m sorry, but marriage is simply out of the question! Good day, sir!”

Smaller Roles Scene

APPLE VENDOR: “Go away, filthy beggars!” (The BEGGARS start to leave, but ALADDIN stops them. He holds out the bread.) ALADDIN: “Go ahead. Take it. I wasn’t that hungry anyway.” (The BEGGARS take the bread and begin devouring it, gratefully.) BEGGAR 2: “Thanks, Aladdin!” BEGGAR 3: “We can always count on you.”
